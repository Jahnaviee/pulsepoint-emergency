First Aid Guide

Simple, essential information to help you respond to common injuries and emergencies while professional medical help is arranged.

Remember: Make sure the scene is safe first. Call emergency services for life-threatening conditions and follow the dispatcher's instructions. First aid is not a substitute for professional care.

BASIC EMERGENCY RESPONSE

Essential First Aid Instructions

01

Unresponsive Person

If someone collapses or does not respond, act quickly.

  1. Check that the area is safe to approach.
  2. Check whether the person responds and is breathing normally.
  3. Call 112 in India or ask someone nearby to call.
  4. If the person is not breathing normally, begin CPR if you can and follow the emergency dispatcher's instructions.
  5. Use an AED if available and follow its voice prompts.

Important: Gasping is not normal breathing. Do not delay CPR while searching for medical supplies.

02

Severe Bleeding

Heavy bleeding can become life-threatening quickly.

  1. Call emergency services for severe or uncontrolled bleeding.
  2. Apply firm, continuous direct pressure with clean gauze or cloth.
  3. If blood soaks through, add more material without removing the original dressing.
  4. For life-threatening limb bleeding, use a commercial tourniquet if available and you are trained, following emergency guidance.
  5. Keep the person warm and monitor their condition until help arrives.

Avoid: Removing deeply embedded objects. Apply pressure around the object and seek urgent medical help.

03

Burns

Prompt cooling can help limit damage from a thermal burn.

  1. Move away from the heat source when safe.
  2. Cool the burn under cool running water for 20 minutes.
  3. Remove jewellery and loose clothing near the area, but not anything stuck to the skin.
  4. Cover loosely with a clean, non-fluffy dressing.
  5. Seek medical attention for large, deep, chemical, electrical, facial, or otherwise serious burns.

Avoid: Ice, butter, toothpaste, oils, or breaking blisters.

04

Possible Stroke

Use FAST to recognize common stroke warning signs.

  1. F — Face: Is one side of the face drooping?
  2. A — Arms: Is one arm weak or drifting down?
  3. S — Speech: Is speech slurred or unusual?
  4. T — Time: Call emergency services immediately.

Note when symptoms began or when the person was last known to be well.

Do not: Give food, drinks, or medication unless directed by a qualified professional.

05

Choking

Severe choking may prevent a person from speaking, coughing effectively, or breathing.

  1. If the person can cough forcefully, encourage coughing and monitor them.
  2. If they cannot speak, breathe, or cough effectively, call emergency services.
  3. For a conscious adult or child over one year, give up to five back blows, then up to five abdominal thrusts if needed.
  4. Repeat the sequence while waiting for help, following dispatcher instructions.
  5. If the person becomes unresponsive, lower them safely and begin CPR as instructed.

Note: Infants under one year need a different technique. Do not perform abdominal thrusts on an infant.

06

Seizures

Protect the person from nearby hazards and allow the seizure to pass.

  1. Move dangerous objects away and cushion the head if possible.
  2. Time the seizure and stay with the person.
  3. Do not restrain their movements or put anything in their mouth.
  4. When the movements stop, check breathing and place them on their side if appropriate.
  5. Call emergency services if the seizure lasts five minutes or longer, repeats without recovery, causes injury, occurs in water, or is a first known seizure.

Remember: Do not give food, water, or oral medication until the person is fully alert.

BE PREPARED

Build a Basic First Aid Kit

Keep essential supplies together and check them regularly.

🩹 Dressings

Sterile gauze pads, adhesive bandages, medical tape, and clean dressings.

🧤 Protection

Disposable gloves, hand-cleaning supplies, and a small pair of scissors.

📋 Emergency Details

Emergency contacts, relevant medical information, and a torch with working batteries.

When in Doubt, Get Professional Help

First aid can support someone while help is on the way, but it cannot replace medical assessment. If the person is seriously unwell or their condition is worsening, contact emergency services immediately.
